# Depth: 2.5D layers and cameras

**Goal:** layers move and turn in 3D space and are seen through a camera, as [D58](../design/decisions.md#d58-25d-layers-are-planes-projected-through-a-camera) says. The import can then carry a project's 3D layers and cameras instead of drawing them flat:

```json
{"motion":1, "size":"1080p",
 "layers":[
   {"id":"cam", "camera":{"zoom":2666.7}, "at":[960,540], "z":-2666.7, "target":[960,540,0]},
   {"id":"card", "rect":"600x400", "3d":true, "at":[960,540],
    "anim":{"rotate-y":"-90 -> 0 @0-0.6s out-cubic", "z":"400 -> 0 @0-0.6s"}}]}
```

## What the corpus says

Counts come from the 198 local sample projects, read with `motion inspect`:

- **Reach.**
  - 1,052 layers in 66 projects are 3D.
  - 95 cameras are in 39 projects; 12 comps hold more than one camera.
  - 73 cameras have a parent, mostly a null that orbits or dollies.
  - 23 projects turn layers about X or Y, and 18 set an orientation.
  - 443 3D layers in 41 projects are collapsed comps, so their layers share the parent's camera.
  - 138 text layers are 3D; none use per-character 3D.
  - 3 projects have lights.
  - 14 cameras turn on depth of field.
- **Effect on fidelity.** In the last full run, "3D layers (drawn flat)" was the first blocker, reported in 53 measured samples with a summed settled error of 16.8, and cameras the ninth (28 samples). Some samples report nothing, yet have the highest errors of the corpus: m572 (0.93), m575 (0.90), m574 (0.63). They hold a camera in a nested comp that the report missed.

## The format

- **The switch.** `"3d":true` makes a layer a plane in the composition's 3D space. A layer without it stays flat, drawn in screen space as today.
- **Transform rows.** They are new layer properties, all animatable, all resting at zero except scale:
  - `z`, the depth of `position` in px, away from the viewer as it grows;
  - `anchor-z`;
  - `scale-z`, which only children and the depth of their positions feel;
  - `rotate-x` and `rotate-y`, in degrees; `rotate` stays the turn about Z;
  - `orient`, three angles in degrees (`"orient":"0,90,0"`). Its keys interpolate along the shortest turn between them (spherical), not angle by angle.

  On a flat layer they are an error from validation, not ignored.
- **Order.** The layer's transform is: move the anchor to the origin, scale, turn about Z, then Y, then X, then orient, then move to the position. As a matrix product that is `T(position) · O · Rx · Ry · Rz · S · T(−anchor)`, with the parent's transform before it.
- **Cameras.** A `camera` layer (`"camera":{"zoom":2666.7}`) is the viewpoint for its composition's 3D layers.
  - `zoom` is the distance in px at which a plane at the camera's focus shows at 100 %.
  - `target`, a point with depth, makes it a two-node camera: it turns to look at the target, after its own rotations.
  - Without `target`, it looks along its own +Z.
  - The topmost camera shown at a frame is the one used. A composition without one uses the default camera. That camera has a 39.6° horizontal view, is centred, and sits at `−zoom` with `zoom = width / (2·tan 19.8°)`. For 1920 px, that is 2,666.7 px.
  - A camera draws nothing. It can be a parent, and layers can be parented to it.
- **Nested compositions.** A `comp` layer draws its composition flat, through that composition's own camera. When it is 3D itself, that drawing is a plane in the parent's space. A collapsed composition's 3D layers join the parent's 3D space and its camera, as they do in the projects (D54 left this open).
- **Mixing flat and 3D layers.** Layers stay in list order. A run of adjacent 3D layers is drawn back to front by the depth of each layer's centre in camera space. A flat layer between two runs ends the first run and starts the next one.

## Drawing

- **Projection.** A 3D layer's own coordinates map to the canvas through a 3×3 projective matrix: its world matrix, then the camera's view and the perspective. The matrix is applied to the plane `z = 0` of the layer's space.
- **Rasteriser.** `motion-raster` gains projected layers (`Layer::projection`):
  - The layer's content, masks and effects are drawn into its own buffer, in its own coordinates, as an isolated layer is.
  - The buffer covers the part of its bounds that the canvas sees.
  - It is drawn at the highest magnification the projection reaches there, capped at 4× and at 8,192 px a side.
  - It is then laid onto the canvas pixel by pixel through the inverse matrix.
  - Sampling is trilinear over a mip chain, picked from the local footprint, so tilted planes neither alias nor blur.
  - Pixels whose ray meets the plane behind the camera are not drawn, so a plane that crosses the camera is cut at it with no polygon clipping.
- **Blend, matte and opacity** apply after the projection, in canvas space, as for a flat layer.
- **Motion blur** ([D57](../design/decisions.md#d57-motion-blur-samples-a-layers-motion-over-the-shutter)) samples the projection over the shutter, as it samples a transform.

## The import

- **Switches.** The 3D switch of `ldta` becomes `3d`.
- **Transform.**
  - Position and anchor keep their Z as `z` and `anchor-z`, scale its Z as `scale-z`.
  - X and Y rotation map to `rotate-x` and `rotate-y`, orientation to `orient`.
  - A 3D position whose spatial path bends in depth is keyed per frame from our own evaluation of the 3D path, as expressions are baked: the scene's spatial tracks are 2D.
- **Cameras.**
  - A camera layer becomes `camera`, with its zoom.
  - Its anchor point is the point of interest, which becomes `target` when the layer auto-orients towards it.
  - The defaults of a left-out zoom and position are measured against the references before they are written down.
- **Reported, not drawn:** lights and their shadows, materials, depth of field, intersecting planes (drawn by depth order, not split), and the 3D renderers other than the classic one.

## Steps

Each step is one pull request, with its tests and docs.

1. **Decision.** D58 and this plan.
2. **Format and evaluation.** The rows, the orientation type with its spherical keys, camera layers, `target`, validation, and each node's world matrix in evaluation (`glam` for the 4×4 and quaternion maths). Camera state and the default camera.
3. **Projected layers in the rasteriser.** `Layer::projection`, the buffer region and scale, mip chain, trilinear sampling and the behind-camera cut. Golden tests for a card flip, a plane crossing the camera, a far tilted plane (aliasing) and a projected layer with effects and a mask.
4. **The engine.** Runs of 3D layers, depth sorting, the camera of each composition, collapsed compositions joining the parent's space, motion blur through projections, and `check` and `at` seeing projected bounds.
5. **The import.** Switches, rows, cameras, the defaults measured against the references, and a measurement on the corpus.
6. **Docs.** Scope, rendering and the plan as built.

## Out of scope

- Lights, shadows, materials and extrusion (phase 2, D1).
- Depth of field.
- Splitting planes that intersect.
- Per-character 3D text.
