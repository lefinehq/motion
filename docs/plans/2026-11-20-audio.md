# Audio

Scenes have no sound ([D8](../design/decisions.md#d8-basic-audio-in-v1) promised basic audio), and audio-driven animation was out of v1 ([Scope](../design/scope.md)). Both are now in: music is part of every film the product makes, and templates draw spectrums and waveforms from their music (`ADBE AudSpect` is drawn as nothing in 9 corpus samples today; none uses `ADBE AudWave`). This plan places, trims, fades and mixes sound into the output, and lets effects and animation read it ([D56](../design/decisions.md#d56-audio-layers-mixed-at-48-khz-and-read-by-effects)).

## Design

**The layer.** `"audio":"music.mp3"` plays a sound file, relative to the scene file. An audio layer draws nothing.

- **Time.** It plays in the layer's own time, as a video does: at its own time `t` it plays the sound `t` seconds into the file. `offset` starts it later, `span` trims it, and `stretch` plays it slower or faster, as tape would, pitch included. Before 0 and from the file's end on it is silent.
- **Volume.** `volume` is a gain, 1 as recorded, 0 silent, up to 4 (+12 dB). It is a property like `opacity`, so fades are keys: `"anim":{"volume":"0 -> 1 @0-1s"}`. A `comp` layer's `volume` scales everything its composition plays, as a precomposition's audio levels do.
- **Muting.** A `hidden` audio layer is silent, as a hidden layer is out of the frame.
- **Nesting.** Audio layers inside a composition play while the `comp` layer showing it is in its span, in that composition's time.

**The mix.** One stereo track at 48 kHz, 32-bit float. Each file is decoded once per session, whole, then resampled to 48 kHz and laid out as stereo. Mono goes to both sides; more channels fold down with the standard coefficients. A layer's volume is evaluated by the scene's own curves every millisecond and ramped linearly in between, so fades have no steps. Layers are summed, with no limiter; a render reports samples above full scale.

**The crate.** `motion-audio` reads sound files and mixes; it knows nothing about scenes, as `motion-video` does not.

- **Formats** behind one `Decoder` trait, chosen by the file's first bytes, not its name:
  - WAV (PCM 8, 16, 24 and 32 bits, float 32 and 64, `WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE`): our own reader.
  - MP3 through `nanomp3`, FLAC through `claxon`, Ogg Vorbis through `lewton`: pure Rust, permissive licences.
  - AAC (`.m4a`, `.mp4`, and the sound of video files) from the operating system, as H.264 is ([D13](../design/decisions.md#d13-codec-licence-policy)), with the track found by `motion-video`'s container reader.
- **Resampling** through `rubato`'s sinc resampler at fixed settings, so the same file gives the same samples on every run.

**Output.** `motion render scene.json --out mix.wav` writes the mix alone. MP4 and MOV get an audio track: AAC from the operating system where it has an encoder, PCM in MOV. That step changes `motion-encode`, so it is agreed on the board with the encode session first.

**Reading sound.**

- **Effects.** `audio-spectrum` and `audio-waveform` read an audio layer of the same list, as `displacement-map` reads a layer (`ItemKind::input`). At the layer's time they read the window of samples the effect asks for, take its spectrum (our own radix-2 FFT under a Hann window, which keeps the dependency list short and every machine on the same numbers) or its waveform, and draw bars, lines or dots along a line or a path. Every frame is computed alone, so any frame renders on its own.
- **Animation.** A property can follow a sound's level, as converting audio to keyframes does: a modifier next to `jitter` and `loop` ([D10](../design/decisions.md#d10-modifiers-instead-of-scripts)), designed in its step.

**Import.** Audio footage beside the project becomes an audio layer, with its audio levels as `volume`. `ADBE AudSpect` and `ADBE AudWave` become the effects, reading the layer the project names. A template whose sound comp is empty still draws nothing there, as the original does.

## Steps

Each step is one pull request, with its tests.

1. **`motion-audio`: reading sound.** The `Decoder` trait, WAV, MP3, FLAC and Vorbis, the fold-down to stereo and the resampler; `Sound`, a file decoded at 48 kHz.
2. **Audio layers and the mix.** The `audio` layer and `volume` in the format, the scene and the engine; `Session::mix`; `motion render --out mix.wav`.
3. **Sound in the output.** An audio track in MP4 and MOV, after the encode session agrees; AAC from the operating system.
4. **AAC input.** `.m4a` files and the sound of video files, through the container reader and the operating system's decoder.
5. **Spectrum and waveform effects.**
6. **Import of audio layers**, their levels and switch, and of the spectrum effect, which reads them. (The effect's import moved here from step 5: it names a layer only this step writes.)
7. **Following a sound's level** in animation.
8. **Docs.** Scope, rendering, the format, the CHANGELOG as built.

## Tests

- Each format decodes small files made for the tests to the samples an independent decoder gives, exactly for WAV and FLAC and within a tolerance for MP3 and Vorbis.
- The resampler keeps a sine's frequency and level, and gives the same samples twice.
- A scene mixes a layer at its offset, trimmed by its span, stretched, faded by `volume` keys, muted when hidden, and inside a composition at that composition's time.
- A spectrum of a sine has its peak in the sine's band, at any frame drawn alone.
