# Effects

After fonts and footage, effects are what imported scenes miss most. Measured on the local corpus of 196 samples (2026-11-16), the effects that block the most samples are:

| Family | Effects (samples) |
|---|---|
| Adjustment layers | 87; they apply their effects to everything below and draw nothing today |
| Transform | Transform 33 (342 uses) |
| Colour | Tint 25, Curves 21, Hue/Saturation 13, Exposure 8, Levels 7 |
| Blur-based | Drop Shadow 38, Glow 26, Box/Fast/Gaussian/Lens/Directional blur about 12 each, Sharpen 10 |
| Generators | Ramp 35, Grid 15, Fractal Noise 12, 4-Color Gradient 11, Venetian Blinds 13 |
| Distortion | Motion Tile 21, Turbulent Displace 16, Displacement Map 12, Offset 5 |
| Particles and audio | Particle World 8, Particle Systems 7, Audio Spectrum 9 |
| Pseudo effects | 16; controls only, nothing to draw |

The registry ([D34](../design/decisions.md#d34-a-layer-tree-renderer-on-vello_cpu)) already joins a scene effect to a draw function by name. These steps fill it, one pull request each, each measured against `main` on the samples it touches.

## Steps

1. **Quick fixes.**
   - The transform effect.
   - Pseudo effects as controls.
   - Sparse generators the scene cannot draw leave their layer drawing nothing, rather than its solid.
   - Effect points in pixels.
2. **Adjustment layers** as filter layers: a layer with `fx` and no content applies its effects to what is below it in its composition.
3. **Colour:** Tint, Curves, Hue/Saturation, Levels, Exposure. Each is a function per pixel, behind one pass over the layer's pixels.
4. **Blur:** one Gaussian kernel, and on it Gaussian, Fast and Box blur, Drop Shadow, Glow and Sharpen.
5. **Generators:** Ramp, 4-Color Gradient, Grid, Venetian Blinds, Fractal Noise.
6. **Distortion:** Motion Tile, Offset, Displacement Map, Turbulent Displace.
7. **Particles** last. They are costly, and each system is its own model.

## Pixels

Effects other than moves need the pixels of what their layer drew. A layer with such an effect is drawn on its own canvas the size of the frame, as a matte is today. Its effects then run in order over the pixels, and the result is drawn back in the layer's place, through its opacity, blend mode and matte. Effects run on the layer as it lands on the frame: an effect's lengths and points are given in the layer's own coordinates and are carried through the layer's transform. That is exact for moves, turns and uniform scales, which is how imported layers are placed.

## Exactness

Most effects have no published formula, and no reference renders each effect on its own. Each effect starts from a public, permissively licensed implementation of the same effect. Its parameter mapping, such as a blur radius to a standard deviation, is then measured on corpus samples where that effect dominates the frame, and the measurement goes in that step's pull request.

## As built

### Step 6 (2026-11-16)

- **Distortions** (`motion-raster/src/distort.rs`): each works out, for every pixel, the point of the layer's own coordinates it shows, and reads the drawing there.
  - `motion-tile`, `wrap` (the project's Offset; `offset` is a property name of other kinds), and `turbulent-displace` with all nine of its kinds:
    - turbulent, and turbulent smoother;
    - one axis (horizontal, vertical) or cross;
    - bulge along the noise's slope, and twist round its hills.
  - The first try mapped only turbulent. m606's horizontal displacement then drew as turbulence, worse than nothing (0.203 → 0.241 over all frames), which is why every kind came in.
- **The layer's box:** the engine gives each raster layer its box (`Layer::bounds`, `Space::bounds`): a rect's, an image's or video's frame, a composition's canvas. Tiles, wraps and pins use it.
- **Effects that read another layer:**
  - An effect kind may name a layer of the same list (`ItemKind::input`; `displacement-map` calls it `map`).
  - The engine draws that layer in its own coordinates, with its effects but not its transform, opacity or matte, as a project reads a layer parameter. A hidden map layer still draws for it.
  - Validation refuses a layer that reaches itself through what its effects read and its matte.
  - The import reads the layer a parameter names from its `tdpi` (`Value::Layer`).
- **Measured** against step 5 on 193 samples: mean settled content error 0.2956 → 0.2956 (2 better, 2 worse).
  - Best: m569 0.850 → 0.842. Worst: m820 0.354 → 0.362.
  - Over all frames 5 better, 8 worse, the largest m750 0.631 → 0.658 and m578 0.506 → 0.530.
  - Distortions move what is there. Where the layers under them are placeholders for missing footage, or the frames measured sit outside the effect, they change little or for the worse.

### Step 5 (2026-11-16)

- **Generators** (`motion-raster/src/generate.rs`): `ramp`, `four-color`, `grid`, `venetian-blinds`, `fractal-noise`.
  - Each is worked out at every pixel in the layer's own coordinates, so it moves with the layer, and draws where the layer is, by its alpha: a solid's box, the usual canvas of a generator.
  - `mode` draws a generator alone (`replace`, a project's "None") or over the layer (`over`, its "Normal"). Other blending modes are reported.
  - `four-color` weighs its colours by inverse distance to the power 2 × 100 / `blend`, our reading of the project's blend.
  - `fractal-noise` is our own value noise, the same kind but not the same pixels: octaves, sub-scaling, sub-influence and sub-rotation, evolution through a third dimension, contrast, brightness and overflow. A cell is 100 px at a scale of 1.
- **Import:**
  - The project's generators map with their points in px of the layer.
  - Fractal Noise switches between a uniform scale and a width and height apart, as the transform effect does.
  - Grid leaves the list of effects that draw in place of their layer.
  - Scatter, jitter, feather, perspective offset and cycled evolution are reported when set.
- **Settings:** a setting name must not be a kind's name. Noise inversion is `polarity`, because `levels` is a kind.
- **Measured** against step 4 on 193 samples: mean settled content error 0.3172 → 0.2956 (32 better, 5 worse), the largest step so far. Gradient and noise solids no longer stay flat colour.
  - Best: m826 0.854 → 0.074, m804 0.709 → 0.110, m775 0.412 → 0.010, m790 0.286 → 0.059, m559 0.259 → 0.069.
  - Worst settled: m566 0.336 → 0.473. A full-frame solid that the reference does not show was red and is now its gradient; it should not be visible at all, which is a separate fault.
  - Over all frames, m579 0.203 → 0.681 and m580 0.305 → 0.537 now draw a grid composition that their H.264 footage covers in the reference.

### Step 4 (2026-11-16)

- **One kernel:** an extended box pass whose radius is fractional averages the pixels around each, plus the two beyond them by the fraction, so an animated blur grows smoothly. Three passes with the radius chosen for a variance give a Gaussian of that variance (Gwosdek et al., 2011), in time that does not grow with the blur (`motion-raster/src/blur.rs`). A test checks the variance against the Gaussian's within 1%.
- **Effects:**
  - `gaussian-blur` is Gaussian Blur and Fast Blur.
  - `box-blur` is Fast Box Blur: `iterations` passes of `radius`.
  - `drop-shadow`: the alpha moved, coloured and blurred, under the layer or alone.
  - `glow`: colours past a luma threshold, blurred and added.
  - `sharpen`: a 3×3 unsharp mask.
  - Lengths are in the layer's px and scale with its transform; `dimensions` and `edges` are settings.
- **Blurriness to σ:** 0.3, the factor public renderers of such projects use.
  - The corpus could not refine it. Every blur length was scaled from 0.5× to 2× on 12 samples, measuring the mean RGB error of settled frames:
    - m548 (0.205 → 0.167) and m784 (0.410 → 0.401) do better with longer blurs.
    - m798 (0.085 → 0.102) and m786 (0.134 → 0.140) do better with shorter ones.
    - The other eight do not move: their blurs sit outside the measured frames or under footage.
    - The samples disagree, and each has its own reason to be off, so the factor stays.
  - It is one constant, `SIGMA_PER_BLURRINESS`, to revisit with a project rendered for it.
- **Import:**
  - 8-bit levels (a shadow's opacity, a glow's threshold) become fractions through `Convert::Level`.
  - A glow based on alpha, with an operation other than add, or with its own colours is reported.
- **Measured** against step 3 on 193 samples: mean settled content error 0.3179 → 0.3172 (13 better, 5 worse); over all frames 16 better, 6 worse.
  - Best: m607 0.452 → 0.322 over all frames; settled m548 0.265 → 0.183, m798 0.080 → 0.055.
  - Worst: m786 0.117 → 0.138. Its glow and shadows are drawn, but its reference frame is black where the scene draws a slate background, so the added light counts against it. m760 0.030 → 0.036 is a glow on text.

### Step 3 (2026-11-16)

- **Effects:** `tint`, `black-white`, `hue-saturation`, `exposure`, `levels`, `curves` and `invert`, each a pass over the straight colour of a layer's pixels (`motion-raster/src/colour.rs`).
  - Settings reach the rasteriser as choice parameters, after the properties.
  - A path property reaches it as a curve (`motion_raster::Curve`): up to 16 points joined by a natural cubic spline, kept as a 256-level table as 8-bit tools keep it.
  - Black & White was not in the plan. m736's adjustment layers run it before their Curves, and Curves alone made the frame worse, so it came with them.
- **Curves data:** a project keeps a Curves effect's points next to its valueless parameter, in `LIST:aRbs` → `aRbp`:
  - a 4-byte head, then five 256-level tables (RGB, red, green, blue, alpha);
  - then five records of 16 big-endian `u16` (input, output) pairs, a `u32` count and an `i32` selection.
  - The natural cubic spline through the points, rounded, gives the stored tables exactly (checked on three projects, largest difference 0).
  - The decoder reads the data as `Value::Data`; the import writes the points as paths.
- **Measured** against step 2 on 193 samples: mean settled content error 0.3183 → 0.3179 (9 better, 3 worse); over all frames 0.3530 → 0.3527.
  - Best: m594 0.311 → 0.243, m563 0.120 → 0.078, m614 0.430 → 0.400.
  - Worst: m736 0.524 → 0.585. Its black and white grade is right, but it lands on a grey placeholder where the reference has H.264 footage, which the curves brighten. m595 0.402 → 0.429 has the same cause, with Sharpen and Noise missing too.
  - Many tints and curves sit on adjustment layers over footage the scene cannot draw yet, so the gain is small until H.264 lands.

### Step 2 (2026-11-16)

- **Pixels** ([D49](../design/decisions.md#d49-effects-that-read-pixels-run-on-a-canvas-of-their-own)):
  - Effect rows are `Apply::Move` or `Apply::Pixels { over, pixels }`.
  - A layer with an effect that reads pixels is drawn on a canvas of its own. Moves before that effect still fold into drawing.
  - `Pixels` holds premultiplied RGBA as floats, with bilinear `sample`, `transformed` and `mix`.
- **Adjusting layers:**
  - `"adjust": true` applies a layer's `fx` to what is below it in its composition, where its content's alpha is, mixed in by its opacity and matte. A layer with only `fx` is a filter layer and applies everywhere.
  - The rasteriser draws the siblings below on a canvas, runs the effects, mixes, and lays the result down. Its parent is isolated, so that is all the composition drew.
- **Import:**
  - An adjustment layer keeps its content (its solid, shapes or composition) as its region and gets `adjust`.
  - One without an effect the scene draws is a pivot.
  - The first try applied every adjustment layer everywhere. m651, whose adjustment layer is drawn from a composition, went from 0.339 to 0.437, which is why the region is the content.
- **Measured** against step 1: mean settled content error 0.3184 → 0.3183 (1 better, 5 worse by under 0.04).
  - Adjustment layers mostly carry effects the scene does not draw yet: Curves, Tint, Glow, blurs. Steps 3 and 4 draw them, through this path.
  - Today only fill and transform reach the frame this way. The largest change is m635, 0.719 → 0.636. The worst is m614, 0.390 → 0.430: a transform on an adjustment layer over H.264 footage the scene cannot draw.

### Step 1 (2026-11-16)

- **`transform` effect:** a group's transform (`position`, `anchor`, `scale`, `rotate`, `opacity`) applied to everything the layer draws. In the rasteriser, an effect row either paints over the drawn content (`Apply::Paint`) or moves it before it is drawn (`Apply::Move`). Moves fold into the content's transform in order. Every painting effect so far works pixel by pixel, so a move commutes with them.
- **Effect points:** the project stores an effect's points in fractions of the layer's size, its source's or else its composition's. The import's `Convert::Sized` and the bake's per-component units turn them into pixels, both ways for export. Speeds and clamps go per component.
- **Uniform scale:** a kind can carry a switch, a checkbox that picks another set of rows. The transform effect's uniform scale writes one number; otherwise each axis gets its own.
- **Pseudo effects** (`Pseudo/…`) are expression controls.
- **Sparse generators:** a layer whose grid, beam, particles or audio shape the scene cannot draw is a pivot, reported as `layers whose `<effect>` draws in their place (drawn as nothing)`. Gradients and noise first went this way too, and frames got worse: they are usually full-frame backgrounds, and their solid is closer to the gradient than nothing is. They keep drawing their solid until step 5 draws them.
- **Measured** on the corpus against `main` (193 samples measured):
  - Mean settled content error falls from 0.3575 to 0.3184; 28 samples are better, 4 worse and 161 the same.
  - Most improved:
    - m845: 0.932 → 0.076
    - m799: 0.716 → 0.025
    - m612: 0.899 → 0.163
    - m611: 0.713 → 0.260, its audio spectrum's white solid gone
    - m563: 0.636 → 0.120
  - Worse: m587 (0.376 → 0.640), m815 (0.222 → 0.242) and m589 (0.653 → 0.654). In each, a beam or particle solid stood in front of footage the scene cannot draw yet (H.264), and happened to be closer to it than nothing.
