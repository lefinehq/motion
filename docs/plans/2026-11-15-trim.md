# Trim: drawing outlines on

The third step of the motion vocabulary. Drawing a line on is a staple of motion design, and the projects we import use it: trim paths appear 895 times in 65 of the 241 local sample projects. Today the import reports them as unsupported and draws the whole outline.

## Design

**The item.** `trim` is a shape item that cuts the outlines before it down to a stretch of their length:

- `end`: where the stretch ends, as a share of the length from 0 to 1. It is the kind key: `{"trim": 0.5}`. Rest 1.
- `start`: where it starts, 0 to 1. Rest 0.
- `offset`: shifts the stretch along the outline, in degrees; 360° is once around. Rest 0.
- `mode`: `each` cuts every outline the same way on its own (the default). `joined` treats the outlines as one line, in order, and cuts that.

**Which outlines.** A trim cuts every outline listed before it in its group, the outlines inside earlier groups included. Within a group, trims apply in order, and those of an inner group apply before those of the groups around it.

A trim changes the outline itself, not a paint: every fill and stroke that draws the outline draws the cut one, wherever it sits in the list. This is how the project's trim paths behave, and why a single trim after a set of groups draws all their lines on.

**The stretch.**
- If start is after end, they swap.
- The offset moves both. A stretch that runs past the end of a closed outline goes on around it as one line. On an open outline it continues from the beginning as a second piece.
- When start equals end nothing is drawn at all, so a round or square cap leaves no dot.
- A whole outline (0 to 1, any offset on a closed outline) stays as it is.
- A cut closed outline is open, so a stroke ends with its cap. A fill closes it with a straight line, as any open outline is filled.

**Lengths** are arc lengths, measured in the space where each outline sits (its group's).

**Import.**
- `ADBE Vector Filter - Trim` becomes `trim`:
  - Start and End are percentages and become fractions.
  - Offset stays in degrees.
  - Trim Multiple Shapes maps 1 (simultaneously) to `each` and 2 (individually) to `joined`.
- The fidelity ledger gains a sample that draws lines on, from a project with a preview.

## Tests

- **Cuts:** a cut of a straight line, a rectangle and an ellipse has the expected length and end points.
- **Stretch rules:** start after end swaps; an offset wraps a closed outline into one piece and an open one into two; start equal to end draws nothing.
- **Scope:** an outer trim cuts the outlines of an inner group and the strokes inside it, and `joined` spreads one stretch over several outlines in order.
- **Corpus:** the compat corpus covers `trim` and both modes; an import snapshot covers the mapping; the ledger gets its numbers.

## As built

Built as designed, with these notes:

- **One walk, paints last.** `motion-scene` collects every outline once, in its own group's space, and walks the items in order: a trim cuts the outlines gathered so far in place, and each paint records which outlines it covers. The paints' paths are built after the walk, so a paint listed before a trim draws the cut outline too.
- **Whole and empty** are decided from the width `end - start` before the offset moves the stretch, so a full stretch with any offset keeps a closed outline closed, and round-off never opens it.
- **Joined wraps.** Past the end of a joined line, the stretch goes on from the first outline as a separate piece: only a single closed outline wraps as one line.
- **Import.** Trim Multiple Shapes 1 maps to `each` and 2 to `joined`; start and end come in as fractions and offset in degrees. Two fixtures of the import tests use trims and now draw them.
- **Fidelity.** The ledger gains s562, a logo reveal whose lines draw on with 9 trims. Measured against its reference before and after this step:

  | | All frames, failing | From 4 s, failing |
  |---|---|---|
  | Before (trims ignored) | 69.6% | 76.0% |
  | After | 4.2% | 0.27% |

  What is left is what the import reports: stars, zigzag, drop shadows, motion blur and the logo footage.
