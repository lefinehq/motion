# Stagger on text parts

The fourth step of the motion vocabulary. Parts of a text that enter one after another are the most common text motion there is. The scene format has promised `"in":"rise word stagger 3f"` from the start, but nothing reads it. Text animators can already sweep a range across the parts, but a sweep eases the range, not each part: a part cannot overshoot with `out-back` or settle with a spring on its own.

## Design

**Each part runs the animator late.** A text animator gains a turn for each part. Part `k` sees the animator, its selectors included, as it is `delay(k)` frames earlier. Whatever the animator does over time — keys, curves, springs, a moving range — each part does it in turn, with its own curve.

- `stagger` (property, frames, rest 0): how long each part waits after the one before it in the order. At 0 every part moves together, as today.
- `by` (setting: `char`, `char-no-space`, `word`, `line`; default `char`): which parts take turns.
- `order` (setting; default `first`): who goes first.
  - `first`: in reading order, so part `k` waits `k` turns.
  - `last`: the reverse.
  - `center`: outward from the middle; the wait is the distance from the middle part, `|k - (n-1)/2|`, so with an even count the two middle parts go together, half a turn in.
  - `edges`: inward from both ends.
  - `random`: a shuffle of the reading order, the same on every render, picked by `seed`.
- `seed` (property, rest 0): which shuffle `random` takes.

The stagger, order and seed are read at the frame being drawn, without delay. A glyph that is in no part of the count, such as a space under `word`, sees the animator without delay.

**The behaviour shorthand.** A layer's `in` on a text layer takes the parts it moves and the spacing:

```json
"in":"fade-up word 30px 10f out-back stagger 3f"
"in":"pop char spring(.5) stagger 2f center"
```

- A part word (`char`, `char-no-space`, `word`, `line`) moves each part of the text by the preset, about each part's own anchor, instead of the whole layer.
- `stagger <time>` sets the spacing. An order word (`first`, `last`, `center`, `edges`, `random`) sets the order.
- `stagger` or an order needs a part word, and parts need a text layer: an error says which.
- The preset's targets become the animator's values: `x` and `y` offsets become `position`, `opacity` stays opacity, `scale` and `rotate` stay. A preset that moves any other property cannot move text parts, and that is an error naming the property.

It compiles to an animator ahead of the layer's own: its values go from the preset's start to rest over the behaviour's frames with its curve, a `range` selector by the same parts chooses all of them, and the stagger and order are set. The scene file keeps the one shorthand ("store the highest level that fits").

**What is not in this step.**

- Grids, distances measured from positions, easing the waits, and jitter (from the study): they come when a scene needs them.
- Stagger on `repeat` copies: it comes with `repeat`.
- Import: a project's text animators have no stagger of this kind; their ranges keep importing as ranges.

## Tests

- Each order gives the expected waits, `random` is a permutation that the seed changes and that repeats exactly.
- A staggered animator moves part `k` exactly as the unstaggered one does `k·stagger` frames earlier, curves and selectors included.
- A space under `word` is not delayed.
- The shorthand parses parts, stagger and order in any order; the errors (no parts, not a text layer, a preset that moves other properties) say what is wrong.
- The compat corpus covers `stagger`, `seed`, `by`, every `order`, and a behaviour with parts.

## As built

Built as designed, with these notes:

- **Where the turns are made.** Each frame, a staggered animator is sampled once more for each part, at the frame minus its wait (`Turns` in `motion-scene`'s `animate`). A glyph reads the animator through its part. The cost is one animator sample per part, only for animators that stagger.
- **The shuffle** is Fisher-Yates driven by SplitMix64, seeded by the seed's bits, so every platform and render agree.
- **The shorthand** reads `stagger` as a two-word clause, so `stagger` at the end without a time is an error about the stagger, not an unknown curve. The part and order words are the animator's own `by` and `order` choices, and a test checks that none of them is also a curve.
- **The desugaring** lives in `motion-format` (`preset::parts_animator`), so a tool can show or expand the animator an entrance makes. An `anim` track on the layer's opacity no longer clashes with an entrance that fades the parts, since the layer itself does not fade.
- **Compat corpus:** `staggers.json` uses every part and order in the shorthand and on authored animators, a seed, a scene preset on parts, and a staggered selector sweep.
