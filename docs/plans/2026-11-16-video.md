# Video layers

Video footage is the largest gap that an import draws as nothing: the corpus report lists it in 56 samples (337 uses). Still pictures already draw ([D41](../design/decisions.md#d41-image-layers-name-their-file-and-carry-their-box)). The footage shipped beside the local sample projects is:

| Codec | Files | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| H.264 (Main and High, 4:2:0, with B-frames) | 164 | stock clips |
| QuickTime Animation (`rle `, RGB and ARGB) | 42 | overlays such as particles, often with alpha |
| Photo JPEG (`jpeg`, 4:2:2) | 11 | clips |
| Flash video (VP6) | 31 | old previews, not footage a template draws |

## Design

**The layer.** `"video":"clip.mov"` draws a video file, relative to the scene file, in the layer's `box`, as an image layer does (D41): the box is required and the frames are stretched to fill it, so the scene stays checkable without its files.

- **Time.** The clip plays in the layer's own time: at its own time `t`, the layer draws the frame that is showing `t` seconds into the clip, so `offset` and `stretch` move and slow it. Before 0 and from the clip's end on, it draws nothing, as footage does in a project.
- **Frames** are decoded when a frame first needs them, by the engine; the rasteriser receives pixels and never reads files, as with pictures.

**The crate.** `motion-video` reads clips without any system library:

- **Container:** our own reader of the ISO base media file format and QuickTime (`.mp4`, `.mov`): the first video track's sample table (`stsd`, `stts`, `ctts`, `stss`, `stsc`, `stsz`/`stz2`, `stco`/`co64`) and its edit list. No crate we may use reads both formats; the ones that do are copyleft.
- **Decoders** behind one trait, so a codec is a module:
  - QuickTime Animation at 24 and 32 bits, each frame updating the lines that changed in the one before, alpha kept as straight alpha.
  - Photo JPEG, each frame a JPEG, through the JPEG decoder pictures already use.
- **Seeking.** A clip keeps its decoder and the frames it decoded last. A frame after the last decoded one within reach decodes forward; any other starts again from the sync sample before it. Render workers ask for frames in rising order, so this stays one pass through the clip.

**Import.** Video footage whose file is found beside the project, in a codec the crate decodes, becomes a video layer; any other stays reported, by codec (`video footage in H.264 (drawn as nothing)`), so the report says what decoding would unlock.

## Later steps

- **H.264**, the most used codec, comes next, with a decision on how to decode it (its own section in the pull request). Done: [D60](../design/decisions.md#d60-h264-footage-decodes-through-the-systems-decoder), see *As built*.
- Image sequences, footage loops and frame-rate overrides from the project's interpretation.
- Audio stays out: scenes have no sound yet.

## Tests

- The container reader finds the samples and their times in small clips made for the tests, in both containers, with an edit list.
- Each decoder matches reference frames decoded independently, exactly for the lossless codec and within a tolerance for JPEG.
- A clip seeks forward and backward to the same frames as a straight pass.
- A scene with a video layer renders the right frame at each time, nothing outside the clip, and through `offset` and `stretch`.
- The import maps a decodable clip to a video layer and reports the others by codec.

## As built

- **Layer** as designed ([D47](../design/decisions.md#d47-video-layers-play-their-clip-in-their-own-time)): `Content::Video` holds the path and box, the evaluated state carries the layer's time in seconds, and the renderer asks the engine for the frame, which hands back a picture or nothing. A clip the engine cannot open or decode is an error at the layer's `video`, naming the file and the codec.
- **Crate.** `motion-video` has the container reader (`container::read`), the `Decoder` trait, QuickTime Animation (bit-exact with an independent decoder on a 1920 by 1080 ARGB clip: 91 frames in about 145 ms) and Photo JPEG (within 4 levels of libjpeg, mean under 0.25). `Clip::frame_at` finds the frame by presentation time, after the edit list's shift, and keeps the last 16 decoded frames.
- **Import.** The engine's footage finder reads each found clip's sample table and passes `Options::videos` (codec name, and whether it decodes) to the import, which stays free of files. Video footage it cannot find is now reported as `video footage it cannot find`, as stills are.
- **Measured** on the local corpus (193 samples): video footage blocked 56 samples (337 uses). Now 72 layers in 10 samples draw. What stays: H.264 in 51 samples (199 uses), clips not found in 5 (38), Flash video in 2 (32). Against `main`, content error once settled falls from 0.375 to 0.312 in m594 and from 0.456 to 0.402 in m595. In m596, more pixels are within tolerance (48.96% failing becomes 47.56%), but the mean error rises from 0.171 to 0.173. The other seven samples do not move: their clips are matte sources, sit in compositions not shown in the measured frames, or are covered by the solid of an effect the scene cannot draw yet.
- **H.264** ([D60](../design/decisions.md#d60-h264-footage-decodes-through-the-systems-decoder)) decodes on macOS through a VideoToolbox decompression session, `motion_videotoolbox::Decoder` (new `decoder.rs` beside the encoder): samples in, in decode order, one picture out for each, 4:2:0 at 8 or 10 bits. In `motion-video`:
  - `h264` reads `avcC` and picks the backend; off macOS it says the clip needs the system's decoder. `avc` reads the sequence parameter set's video signal type and chroma siting, and which samples nothing refers to. `ycbcr` resolves the colour (the `colr` box, then the stream, then BT.709 above standard definition and BT.601 at it, limited range unless tagged full) and converts in fixed point with linear chroma upsampling; `toolbox` joins them.
  - The `Decoder` trait now hands back a `Picture` that becomes RGBA when a frame asks for it, outside the clip's lock; Photo JPEG decodes that way too.
  - Timing: a track without an edit list starts at its first shown frame (152 of the corpus's 174 clips beside H.264 samples have composition offsets and no edit list, so each frame would have shown a frame late, after a blank first one); a time a hair short of a frame's start, as `n / fps` in floating point is, snaps to it (frame 3 of a 23.976 fps clip showed frame 2); decoding a leading picture of an open group starts from the group before.
  - Checks: the binding decodes what the system's encoders make, for H.264 and HEVC, and seeks to a keyframe; ten test clips made with ffmpeg from generated pictures cover times with and without an edit list, B-frames, an open group, a 40-frame group, and six colour tags (box over stream, untagged SD and HD); a clip written by `motion-encode` reads back within 2 levels; ours decodes a corpus clip as ffmpeg does (BT.709, limited range, bilinear chroma) within 0.1 levels on average.
