## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `motion bench` and the brief set (craft lint plan, step 6): 30 briefs in `bench/briefs/`, by use case (ads, launch and feature videos, explainers, social). `motion bench <briefs> --agent <command> --out <folder>` runs the agent on each brief in a folder of its own, then checks and renders the scene it wrote, and records the agent's time, tokens and steps, the render time and the check's findings in `results.jsonl`. It scores nothing ([D22](docs/design/decisions.md#d22-no-ai-judge)).
- Craft lint, step 5 ([D59](docs/design/decisions.md#d59-craft-lint-measures-motion-from-the-scene-and-reports-numbers)): calibration. A planted-defect scene per rule trips that rule and no other (`crates/motion-engine/tests/planted.rs`). Over the local corpus's 31 faithfully drawn templates, `judder` fired on 4, `text_hold` on 3, `crowd` and `contrast` on 1, `easing` and `flash` on none ([Quality](docs/design/quality.md#calibration)). Two limits moved: `judder` counts fast steps only when they last 1/8 s, so the peak of an ease passes (`Craft::lasting`), and `text_hold`'s floor fell from 5/6 s to 0.5 s.
- Craft lint, step 4 ([D59](docs/design/decisions.md#d59-craft-lint-measures-motion-from-the-scene-and-reports-numbers)): the pixel gates. `motion check` now draws the scene at a quarter of its size and fails on `contrast`, a readable text layer whose glyphs reach less than 4.5:1 against what is behind them (3:1 from 24 px; `2.1:1 <4.5`), and `flash`, a quarter or more of the frame flashing more than 3 times a second (`5/s >3`, on `scene`). `Session::gates` runs them; `Session::check` still reads only the scene. `Report::join` adds one report to another. The launch example now counts 12 passing rules under `motion check`.
- The import carries 3D layers and cameras (depth plan step 5): the 3D switch becomes `"3d":true`; a 3D layer's anchor point, position and scale keep their depth as `anchor-z`, `z` and `scale-z` (`To::Split`), its X and Y rotation become `rotate-x` and `rotate-y`, and its orientation `orient`. A position whose path bends in depth is keyed at every frame from our evaluation of the path in space, timed along its length; a straight one keeps its keys, its speed shared out to `position` and `z`. A camera becomes `"camera":true` with its `zoom`; a two-node camera's point of interest becomes `target` and `target-z`, read from the auto-orient bit of `ldta` (`Switches::orient`, `AutoOrient`). A left-out zoom is `width · 50/36` and a left-out position the composition's centre at minus that zoom, measured against the samples (`docs/design/aep-evidence.md`). Depth of field, turning along the path or towards the camera, and a renderer other than the classic one (`Composition::renderer`, from `prin`) are reported. On the local corpus, 685 3D layers in 56 samples and 70 cameras in 31 now import. Of the reports, 999 go ("3D layers (drawn flat)" 748, "3D orientation" 98, "cameras (drawn as nothing)" 70, `ADBE Rotate X` and `Y` 46, 24 expressions on turns that are now baked, 11 light options and 2 depths) and 15 come (depth of field 14, a renderer 1). Rendering stays flat until the rasteriser and the engine project 3D layers (steps 3 and 4).
- Craft lint, step 3 ([D59](docs/design/decisions.md#d59-craft-lint-measures-motion-from-the-scene-and-reports-numbers)): `motion check` warns about `easing`, a move of at least 6 frames whose peak speed is less than 1.4 times its mean (`1 <1.4` at constant speed), and `crowd`, more than 4 layers moving on their own at once faster than 2 px a frame (`6 >4`, on `scene`). Neither prints a fix. The launch example now counts 10 passing rules.
- Craft lint, step 2 ([D59](docs/design/decisions.md#d59-craft-lint-measures-motion-from-the-scene-and-reports-numbers)): `motion check` warns about `judder`, a layer stepping more than 11 px a frame on a 1080-px canvas without motion blur (fix: `mblur=true`), and `text_hold`, text readable for less than its characters at 20 a second or 5/6 s. A finding prints its worst value and the limit it misses (`24px/f >11`, `0.4s <1.3`); `Violation::measure` (`Measure`, `Unit`) replaces `amount_px`. The launch example now counts 7 passing rules.
- 3D layers draw through their composition's camera (depth plan step 4, [D58](docs/design/decisions.md#d58-25d-layers-are-planes-projected-through-a-camera)). Evaluation gives each 3D node its plane on the canvas (`NodeState::plane`), and the engine lays its drawing through it (`Layer::projection`). Each run of adjacent 3D layers that draw is drawn back to front by the camera-space depth of each box's centre; a flat layer that draws keeps runs apart, and pivots, cameras and audio layers split none. A collapsed composition's 3D layers join the outer composition's camera and space. Motion blur of a 3D layer drawn once lays it through its plane at each moment. `check` and the walk measure what a 3D layer covers through the camera, with what is behind it left out (`Visit::plane`, `space::outline`).
- Projected layers in the rasteriser (depth plan step 3, [D58](docs/design/decisions.md#d58-25d-layers-are-planes-projected-through-a-camera)): `Layer::projection` (`Projection`, `Homography`) draws a layer as a plane seen through a camera. Its content, masks, clip and effects are drawn in its own coordinates, into a buffer covering the part of the plane the canvas sees, at the most the projection magnifies it (up to 4×, at most 8,192 px a side). The buffer is laid onto the canvas px by px through the inverse matrix, sampled trilinearly from a chain of halved copies with up to 16 probes along each footprint. Px whose ray meets the plane behind the camera stay clear. Opacity, blend mode and matte apply after, as for a flat layer, and `Projection::samples` blur its motion. Effects say how far they reach (`Reach`, `EffectSpec::reach`, `Effect::reach`). A 60° card flip is 200.86 px wide against 200.85 by hand, a nearly edge-on checkerboard is within 0.032 of a supersampled reference (0.49 point-sampled), and a full-frame 1080p card turned 35° draws in 53 ms on one thread, against 3.6 ms flat.
- 3D layers and cameras in the format and evaluation ([D58](docs/design/decisions.md#d58-25d-layers-are-planes-projected-through-a-camera), depth plan step 2): `"3d":true` makes a layer a plane in its composition's space, with the new rows `z`, `anchor-z`, `scale-z`, `rotate-x`, `rotate-y` and `orient`. `orient` is a new value type: three angles in degrees whose keys turn the shortest way between them, eased over the angle. A `"camera":true` layer has `zoom`, and `target` and `target-z`: when it sets or animates them, it turns to look at that point. A camera without a depth sits at minus its zoom. Evaluation gives each 3D node its matrix of space (`NodeState::space`, from `glam`), and each frame the view of its last camera that shows, or of the default camera, a 50 mm lens on 36 mm film (`FrameState::view`, `motion_scene::space`). `View::plane` gives the projective matrix that lays a node's plane onto the canvas. Depth rows on a flat layer, and camera rows on another layer, are errors. 3D layers still draw flat until the rasteriser and the engine project them (steps 3 and 4).
- Tracks that follow a sound (audio plan step 7): `"scale":"rest -> rest*1.25 @level music 30-150Hz smooth 0.06s out-quad"` goes from its first value at silence to its second at full level. The level is that of an `audio` or `comp` layer of the same list over a frame's window, as the mix plays it: RMS, or the loudest part of a band of the spectrum, smoothed by a one-pole filter. The curve shapes it. The engine measures it once a frame from a first compile's routes and compiles again with the track keyed every frame (`motion_scene::compile_with`, `Levels`, `Hearing`; step `listen`), so any frame evaluates alone. Layer and item tracks may listen; `volume` may not.
- Motion blur ([D57](docs/design/decisions.md#d57-motion-blur-samples-a-layers-motion-over-the-shutter)): a layer with `"mblur":true` is drawn as the average of its drawings at moments over its composition's `shutter` (`"180 -90 16"` by default, `motion_format::shorthand::Shutter`). A layer that moves far takes up to 8 times the samples. A `comp` layer, or one with effects, is drawn once and moved to each moment, up to 2 times the samples, so nested compositions don't multiply the cost. The engine evaluates each canvas at those moments once per frame and adds the samples at `1/n` each in a group of their own, which then takes the layer's blend, opacity and matte.
- The import plays sounds (audio plan step 6): found audio footage becomes an `audio` layer, sniffed for its format by the engine (`motion_import::map::Sound`, `Options::sounds`). Audio levels become `volume`, keyed every frame where they move, since the original moves in decibels. The layer's audio switch, now read from the project (`Switches::audio`, printed by `inspect`), mutes audio layers and composition layers that play a sound. Audio Spectrum becomes `audio-spectrum`, reading its Audio Layer. Audio Waveform still draws nothing until a project shows its parameters.
- `audio-spectrum` and `audio-waveform` effects ([D56](docs/design/decisions.md#d56-audio-layers-mixed-at-48-khz-and-read-by-effects), audio plan step 5): they draw the sound of the audio layer, or of the composition behind the `comp` layer, that `audio` names: a window `duration` ms long about the layer's time, as the mix plays it. The spectrum shows `bands` frequencies from `from` to `to` Hz, from our own FFT, and the waveform `samples` points of the wave, as squares, bars, lines or dots along a line or round a circle, coloured from `inside-color` to `outside-color` across each stroke. An effect kind now says whether its input is read as a drawing or as a sound (`motion_format::item::Input`), and the format rejects a sound read from a layer that plays none and a drawing read from an audio layer. Defaults follow the original effect: `height` 480, `to` 2000 Hz, `side` `both`.
- Audio layers and the mix ([D56](docs/design/decisions.md#d56-audio-layers-mixed-at-48-khz-and-read-by-effects), audio plan step 2): `"audio":"music.mp3"` plays a sound file in the layer's own time. `offset` starts it, `span` trims it, `stretch` plays it slower and lower, and `hidden` mutes it. The new `volume` property (1 as recorded, up to 4) fades it with keys; on a `comp` layer it scales the composition's sound. An audio layer takes no effects or matte and mattes nothing. `motion_scene::routes` finds every audio layer through its compositions, and `Route::at` says what it plays at any frame. `Session::mix` mixes the scene into stereo at 48 kHz: what each layer plays is asked once a millisecond and moved in a straight line in between, and a layer starts on its own sample. `motion render scene.json --out mix.wav` writes the mix and says when it goes above full scale.
- `motion-audio` ([D56](docs/design/decisions.md#d56-audio-layers-mixed-at-48-khz-and-read-by-effects), audio plan step 1): reads sound files, by their first bytes. WAV (PCM 8 to 32 bits, float, A-law, µ-law, extensible, RF64) is our own reader; MP3, FLAC and Ogg Vorbis go through pure Rust decoders. Each file becomes a `Sound`: its channels and where their speakers stand. `Sound::for_mix` folds a sound down to stereo (ITU-R BS.775) and resamples it to 48 kHz with our own Kaiser-windowed sinc kernel (`Kernel`). `wav::encode` writes a mix. Each format matches ffmpeg's decode of the test files: exactly for WAV and FLAC, within 2e-4 for MP3 and 1e-5 for Vorbis. A three-minute 44.1 kHz MP3 decodes in 0.08 s and is laid out for the mix in 0.18 s.
- A layer's `blend` key: how it blends with what is below it in its composition. It takes one of 17 modes (`motion_format::BLEND_MODES`), which the rasteriser already drew, and the import writes the project's blend modes that match one instead of reporting them.
- The import carries motion blur: a layer with its motion blur switch on, in a composition with motion blur enabled, gets `"mblur":true`, and a composition's shutter angle, phase and samples per frame become its `shutter`. On the local corpus, 39 samples ask for motion blur, and none draws farther from its reference.
- The import carries masks: each mask of a layer becomes an item of its `mask` list, its path scaled from the mask space to the layer's px, and its mode and side read from its `mkif` (`MaskSettings`). Keys of a mask path that differ in vertex count are matched to the largest, each shorter path repeating its first vertex. Mask motion blur and a linear feather falloff are reported. On the local corpus, 44 samples had masks the import left out:
  - the mean failing share falls from 76.77% to 75.95%;
  - five samples improve, up to 82.6% → 13.5%;
  - two get worse, where the masked shapes now show what the scene cannot draw yet, such as time remapping.
- Masks ([D55](docs/design/decisions.md#d55-masks-are-items-that-cut-a-layers-content-before-its-effects)): a layer's `mask` list cuts what it draws before its effects. Each mask is an outline in the layer's px with `feather`, `opacity` and `expand`, all animatable, keeping its `inside` or `outside`. The masks combine in order by `mode`: `add`, `subtract`, `intersect`, `lighten`, `darken`, `difference` or `none`. On a filter layer they say where its effects apply. The rasteriser takes them as `Layer::masks` (`LayerMask`, `MaskMode`) and works out their coverage on the frame's grid (`motion-raster/src/mask.rs`).
- `collapse` on a `comp` layer ([D54](docs/design/decisions.md#d54-a-collapsed-composition-draws-into-the-canvas-it-sits-in)): its composition's layers draw straight into the canvas it sits in, unclipped and without the composition's background, and its effects run over that canvas in its coordinates. The import writes it for a collapsed composition and measures the layer's effect points in the parent's box; 66 of 193 corpus samples have one. Mean settled content error 0.2944 → 0.2945: m553 0.333 → 0.308; m544 0.151 → 0.200, where layers now uncut show text its unsupported masks would cut.
- `motion import template.mogrt` and `motion inspect template.mogrt` ([D53](docs/design/decisions.md#d53-a-mogrt-imports-as-its-project-with-its-controls-as-slots)): the engine opens the template, imports the project inside it and makes its panel the scene's slots (`motion_engine::is_template`). The import proposes a role of text on the one text slot whose control's name has its words, `headline $title (from its control's name)`. `motion export` refuses a template as the project to write into.
- A `.mogrt` template's panel becomes the scene's slots ([D53](docs/design/decisions.md#d53-a-mogrt-imports-as-its-project-with-its-controls-as-slots)): with `map::Options::panel`, the composition the template was made from is the scene, and each control is a slot in the panel's order, named and labelled by it (`Group / Name` inside a group). A still property the control drives follows the slot, a transform value the project leaves out at its default too, and an expression control merges with the slot its plain reads already made, read from any composition. A control nothing follows is left out with why, in a report line `template: N controls, M slots`. On the four local templates, 13 of 33 controls become slots, which 27 values follow; the other 20 drive expression controls that their expressions do more than read.
- `motion_import::template::open` reads a `.mogrt` archive ([D53](docs/design/decisions.md#d53-a-mogrt-imports-as-its-project-with-its-controls-as-slots)) into its panel, each control's id, kind, name and value from `definition.json`, and the bytes of the project inside it. Entries are capped at 1 GiB inflated, and a template with no project inside is refused with why. The decoder reads a composition's panel controls, `Composition::panel`, each with the properties it drives by composition, layer and match-name path (`PanelControl`, `ControlKind`, `ControlTarget`). On the four local templates, all 34 controls are found in their project and drive a layer it has.
- Docs for the variant surface: [Variants](docs/design/variants.md) says what roles, locks and batches do today, and the glossary has Batch, Lock and Role.
- `motion batch variants.json` ([D52](docs/design/decisions.md#d52-a-batch-makes-every-variant-from-one-master)): every variant of one master, each its edits applied in order through the locks and then checked; those that pass render one at a time to `out/<id>.mp4` beside their scene. A variant cannot edit `roles` or `locks`. One report line per variant, `--json` for data, `--check` to stop before rendering, exit 1 when any is refused. `Session::variant`, `Session::edit_variant`, `Session::document` and `Session::save_as`, which writes a scene elsewhere with the files it names still found.
- Locks ([D51](docs/design/decisions.md#d51-locks-are-refused-in-the-edit)): a scene's `locks` list roles, layers, slots, `palette` and `typefaces`, and `apply_edit` refuses an edit that touches one, naming the lock: a locked layer, anything inside it, an override or a composition reaching it; a locked slot; a colour or a font family the scene does not have yet. The import gives a master with a logo `["@logo","palette","typefaces"]`, and `motion roles` marks locked roles. `Scene::held_locks` follows each role lock to what it holds, and `motion_format::color` reads a colour in any form.
- The import proposes roles: a layer named `@headline Big Title` takes the role and the id `big-title`, and otherwise a role of the vocabulary (`headline`, `cta`, `logo`, `background`, …) takes the one layer whose id has one of its words. The report lists them, `roles, to confirm: …`, and `Mapping::roles` holds them. On the corpus, 62 of 196 scenes get a proposal.
- Roles ([D50](docs/design/decisions.md#d50-roles-name-what-a-masters-layers-do)): a scene's `roles` name what its layers and slots do, `{"headline":"title","accent":"$accent"}`, checked to name a layer at any depth or a slot of the scene. An edit may target one, `@headline text="Sale"` or `@accent=#2A9D8F`, through `motion_edit::edit_line`, which `Session::edit` now uses. `motion roles` lists each role with what it holds, and `--json` gives the same as data.
- `particles`: a seekable particle system drawn in place of its layer, each particle born at `i / rate` seconds with its own random draws and a closed-form flight under gravity and resistance, so any frame draws alone (`motion-raster/src/particles.rs`). Layers carry their own time to effects (`NodeState::seconds`, `Layer::time`, `Space::time`). The import maps CC Particle Systems II.
- `displacement-map`, the first effect that reads another layer: an effect kind may name a layer of the same list (`ItemKind::input`, here `map`), which the engine draws in its own coordinates, with its effects but not its transform, for the effect (`Effect::with_input`, `Space::input`). Validation keeps a layer from reaching itself through what its effects read and its matte. The import reads a layer parameter's layer (`Value::Layer`, from `tdpi`).
- Distortions: `motion-tile`, `wrap` and `turbulent-displace`, which read a layer's drawing at other points of its own coordinates (`motion-raster/src/distort.rs`). The engine gives each layer its box (`Layer::bounds`, `Space::bounds`): a rect's, an image's or video's frame, a composition's canvas, which tiles, wraps and pins use.
- `motion export` writes a slot that came from an expression control back into the control, through the conversion of the reads it holds, so `$accent=#2A9D8F` then `motion export` patches the colour control. Only a control whose stored value converts to the slot's exactly is written; one parameter read under two spellings is one slot. The local export sweep counts the slots it writes back: 283 over 206 projects, each read back as edited.
- The import turns controls into slots: an expression that only reads a control, `thisComp.layer("Controls").effect("Accent")("Color")`, becomes `"$accent"` when its value never changes, with the slot declared where the reference sees it and holding what most of the control's reads gave, in the scene's units. A layer of controls only that draws nothing is left out. On the 198-template corpus, 1,295 baked values now follow 690 slots in 94 templates, and 213 control layers are left out. A colour slot takes `[r, g, b, a]` as well as `#RRGGBB`, and the import escapes strings the scene would read as references.
- Slot edits and `motion slots`: `motion edit scene.json '$accent=#2A9D8F'` sets a scene slot in the form it is written in, `card-2 $title=Sale` gives one instance its own value, and `$accent=none` removes a slot only when nothing follows it. `motion slots` lists each slot of the scene and its compositions with how many values follow it, and `--json` gives their places, the surface an agent changes to make a variant. `Document::slots` and `Session::slots` return the same.
- A `comp` layer gives its composition's slots values of its own, `"overrides":{"$title":"Sale"}`: each instance resolves the composition's layers with its values first, then sets its layer keys over them, so a key by address wins. Values are read in the outer scope, so `"$title":"$label"` passes a slot on to a composition drawn inside another. An undeclared id or a value that does not fit is an error at `card-2.overrides.$title`. `Diagnostic::unknown` no longer writes an empty list of names.
- Generators: `ramp`, `four-color`, `grid`, `venetian-blinds` and `fractal-noise`, drawn where the layer is in its own coordinates (`motion-raster/src/generate.rs`). The import maps the project's generators of the same kind, so a gradient or noise solid no longer stays a flat colour, and a grid no longer draws nothing.
- Blurs: `gaussian-blur`, `box-blur`, `drop-shadow`, `glow` and `sharpen`, on one family of box passes that three times over make a Gaussian in time independent of its size (`motion-raster/src/blur.rs`). The import maps Gaussian Blur, Fast Blur, Fast Box Blur, Drop Shadow, Glow and Sharpen; 8-bit levels such as a shadow's opacity become fractions (`Convert::Level`).
- Colour effects: `tint`, `black-white`, `hue-saturation`, `exposure`, `levels`, `curves` and `invert`, each a pass over a layer's pixels. Effect settings reach the rasteriser as choice parameters (`ParamValue::Choice`) and paths as curves (`ParamValue::Curve`, `motion_raster::Curve`). The import maps the project's effects of the same names, Curves from the points its data keeps (`Value::Data`).
- Adjusting layers ([D49](docs/design/decisions.md#d49-effects-that-read-pixels-run-on-a-canvas-of-their-own)): `"adjust": true` applies a layer's `fx` to what is below it in its composition, where its content's alpha is; a layer with only `fx` is a filter layer and applies everywhere. The import turns adjustment layers into them. Effects that read pixels run on a canvas of their own (`Apply::Pixels`, `motion_raster::Pixels`, `Space`).
- `slots` on a scene and on its compositions ([D44](docs/design/decisions.md#d44-slots-are-named-values-that-layers-follow)): named values typed `color`, `number`, `point` or `text`, with an optional label. Any value written `"$id"` follows a slot of its composition, then of the scene; `$$` writes a literal dollar. References resolve on a copy before the scene is read, so the file keeps them and nothing after reading changes. A reference to no slot is an error at the value, and a value that does not fit names its slot.
- `motion render --codec prores --out overlay.mov` on macOS: ProRes 4444 with straight alpha in a QuickTime file, for scenes with a clear background; decoded frames match the drawn ones, alpha within one level. `.mov` files hold H.264 and HEVC too; a container refuses a codec it does not hold, and ProRes offers the one quality `master`.
- H.264 and HEVC from the system's VideoToolbox on macOS, where H.264 is now the default codec of `motion render --out x.mp4`: 60 frames of 1080p in about 0.6 s, against 7.8 s for AV1. The new `motion-videotoolbox` crate is the one allowed unsafe code ([D48](docs/design/decisions.md#d48-unsafe-code-lives-only-in-platform-bindings)), with a reason on every block; a test fails when any other crate leaves the workspace's lints. The MP4 writer holds `avc1` and `hvc1` sample entries.
- `motion render --codec` and `--video-quality draft|web|master` for video files; `--timings` shows the `encode` step. AV1 encodes its tiles, fixed by the frame's size, on every core, so the file is the same on any machine. `motion_engine::telemetry::timing::timed` times a caller's own step alongside the engine's.
- `motion render --out launch.mp4` writes a video ([D46](docs/design/decisions.md#d46-video-is-encoded-in-a-crate-of-its-own-behind-one-codec-trait)): AV1 in MP4 on every platform, with nothing else installed. The new `motion-encode` crate turns frames into BT.709 Y′CbCr 4:2:0, compresses them through `rav1e` behind a `VideoEncoder` trait, and writes MP4 behind a `Muxer` trait, streaming packets to disk with the index first. `Session::render_in_order` draws in parallel and hands frames over in order, holding at most two per thread.
- The `transform` effect: a group's transform applied to everything a layer draws, inside the layer, as the import writes the transform effect of a project. The rasteriser's effect rows now either paint over a layer's content (`Apply::Paint`) or move it (`Apply::Move`), and effect parameters take points (`ParamValue::Point`). The import turns effect points, stored in fractions of the layer's size, into pixels, in the bake too; treats pseudo effects as expression controls; and draws nothing for a layer whose particles, beam, grid or audio shape the scene cannot draw, instead of the solid behind it. See the [effects plan](docs/plans/2026-11-16-effects.md).
- Video layers ([D47](docs/design/decisions.md#d47-video-layers-play-their-clip-in-their-own-time)): `"video":"clip.mov"` with a `box` plays a clip in the layer's own time, so `offset` and `stretch` place and retime it, and draws nothing outside it. The new `motion-video` crate reads MP4 and QuickTime clips with no system library and decodes QuickTime Animation (RGB and ARGB) and Photo JPEG. The import turns found clips in those codecs into video layers through `Options::videos` and reports the others by codec (`video footage in H.264`).
- `Address::written`, `Address::written_in_comp`, `Scene::find` and `SceneGraph::find`: a layer named where the document writes it, in the scene or in a composition. An `Address` reads from and writes to JSON as its text.
- `Scene::layer`, `Scene::drawn` and `Scene::addresses`, `Document::layer_json` and `motion_format::overrides`: a layer at any depth as drawn, typed or as JSON, with the overrides on the way set over it. `motion_format::address::key_path` reads a key path on its own.
- `overrides` on a `comp` layer ([D43](docs/design/decisions.md#d43-one-layer-address-and-edits-per-instance)): layer keys by address, set over the layers of the composition it draws for that layer alone, `{"title":{"text":"Sale"},"badge/icon":{"fill":"#E63946"}}`. The result is validated as any layer is, errors point at the inner layer (`card/title.text`), and each such layer compiles to a composition of its own. `Content::Comp` holds a `CompContent`.
- `motion_scene::walk`: every node of an evaluated frame at any depth, in paint order, with its address, its transform in scene space, its opacity, the clip of the compositions around it and whether it draws. `SceneGraph::paths` lists the same nodes for every frame.
- `motion_format::Address` and `Id` ([D43](docs/design/decisions.md#d43-one-layer-address-and-edits-per-instance)): one grammar and printer for a layer at any depth and the keys inside it (`card/title.fx[glow].radius`). The id rule lives in `Id` instead of a hand-written loop. `scene` is reserved: a layer may not take it, and the import gives such a layer `scene-2`.
- Cargo workspace, repository documents and CI.
- `motion-format`: time, colour and size shorthand parsing, and `fmt_num`.
- First slice: scene format, check, at, edit.
- `motion-scene`: compile a scene into a graph of laid-out layers.
- Evaluate a scene graph at any frame.
- `motion-query`: `check` and `at` over a scene graph.
- `motion-edit`: a one-line edit language for scenes.
- `motion-cli`: the `motion` tool with `check`, `at` and `edit` commands.
- Curves as a table of names plus `cubic(x1,y1,x2,y2)` and `hold`, usable on any property.
- A property registry (`x`, `y`, `opacity`, `scale`, `rotate`) with a default and a range for each.
- Presets as data (`fade`, `fade-up`, `fade-down`, `fade-left`, `fade-right`, `pop`, `spin-in`), and a scene `presets` key to add or override them.
- `anim` tracks, as a one-line string or a keyframe list, with a curve per key; `anim` and `in` may not animate the same property.
- `scale` and `rotate` are evaluated, and `check` measures the transformed bounding box.
- Nested edit paths (`card anim.opacity=...`), JSON values in edits, and removal of objects left empty by `none`.
- Tooling: `proptest` parser tests, `insta` snapshots, coverage and `cargo-deny`.
- One error type, `Diagnostic`, with a kind (syntax, unknown name, out of range, missing, conflict, wrong type, invalid, io) that sets the exit code.
- `motion-engine`: a `Session` that is the one entry point for tools, and `motion-cli` now goes through it.
- Logs with `--log <level>`, `--log-format json` and `MOTION_LOG`, and step timings with `--timings`.
- `motion-import`: reads `.aep` projects (items, compositions, footage, layers, the property tree, keyframes, expressions and paths) into a typed model, keeps what it cannot decode yet as opaque values with a reason, and counts everything in an import report.
- `motion inspect <project.aep>` prints a project as a tree with its import report, or both as JSON with `--json`; the engine reads projects with `motion_engine::import`.
- `motion-import` decodes text documents (text, paragraph and character runs with resolved style sheets, point or box frame, and the line layout the project stored) with a `winnow` grammar for their object syntax and serde records over it, and 3D orientation angles; neither is opaque any more.
- `motion_format::grammar::parse_at`, which also hands the failing offset to the explanation; `parse_all` now takes any `winnow` input, bytes included.
- Typed property values (number, point, colour) and the transform set `position` (components `x`, `y`), `anchor`, `scale` (one number or `[x, y]`), `rotate` and `opacity`; any property can rest at a value set by its name on the layer.
- Keys ease in value space: `speed(out,out-reach,in,in-reach)` holds any ease, including a span between equal keys; a point that moves in space follows a bézier path (`"path"` on an object key) at a pace measured along it; other points can take one curve per component.
- Layers can follow a `parent`, show only in their `span`, and run on their own time with `offset` and `stretch`.
- Shape layers: `shapes` lists items (`group`, `rect`, `ellipse`, `path`, `fill`, `stroke`), each written as an object keyed by its kind with its own property registry, settings (fill `rule`, stroke `cap` and `join`), `anim` tracks, optional `id` and `hidden`. Paints cover the outlines before them in their group, through nested groups' transforms; `motion-scene` evaluates them into draws, back to front, and measures their bounds.
- Path values: SVG path data (`M`, `L`, `C`, `Z`) as a property type that tracks morph vertex by vertex.
- Effects: an `fx` list on any layer, in the same item form; `fill` is the first effect.
- Compositions: a scene `comps` map with its own size, clock, length and background, drawn by `comp` layers at the layer's own time; cycles are rejected. `pivot` layers draw nothing and carry a transform for children. Layers can be `hidden`.
- The canonical writer puts each composition's layers one per line too.
- Text animators: `animate` on text layers lists animators whose key holds `range` selectors (by `char`, `char-no-space`, `word` or `line`; `start`, `end`, `offset`, `amount`, `smooth`; six shapes and six modes). Each glyph takes the animator's `position`, `anchor`, `scale`, `rotate`, `opacity`, `fill`, `stroke`, `stroke-width` and `tracking` scaled by how much it is chosen; `motion-scene` evaluates them per glyph, and `check` measures where the visible glyphs are.
- An item kind's key may hold a setting (a selector's `by`) or items of another table (an animator's selectors).
- Rendering: `motion-engine` maps an evaluated frame to `motion-raster`'s display list (layer transforms, opacity and visibility; rect layers; shape fills and strokes with their rules, caps, joins and miters; text runs with fill, stroke and paint order, per glyph under animators; faux bold as a fill-coloured stroke and faux italic as a 12° slant; nested compositions as clipped, isolated layers over their background; `fx` joined by name to the rasteriser's effects). `Session::display_list`, `render`, `render_with`, `frames` and `render_frames`, which draws a range on every thread.
- `motion render <scene> [--frames A-B | --frame N] --out <dir> [--quality fast]` writes `frame-00000.png` files.
- Golden images of whole scenes in `motion-engine` (shapes, text under an animator, faux styles, nested compositions, the fill effect), and a release timing of a 1080x1920 story scene through every step.
- Text style: a scene `fonts` list; on text layers `style` (fill, stroke, stroke width and paint order, tracking, leading, baseline shift, caps, faux bold and italic, italic faces; alignment, indents and paragraph spacing), `runs` over character ranges, `paragraphs`, and `box` for box text. The keys are one table in `motion_format::text`.
- `motion-raster`: draws a frame's display list (layers with transform, opacity, blend mode, clip and effects; filled and stroked paths; glyph runs) into an RGBA image with `vello_cpu`, and writes PNG files. Seventeen blend modes, caps, joins and fill rules are tables; the `fill` effect is the first row of the effect registry. Golden images are drawn with portable scalar code so they compare exactly across machines.
- `motion-text`: a font set read from given files and folders (by PostScript name, or family, weight and slant; every named instance of a variable font; Inter built in), and text layout with Parley and HarfRust: paragraphs, character runs, point and box text, every justification, indents, paragraph spacing, leading, tracking, baseline shift and capitals, into lines with cumulative character advances and glyph runs ready for `vello_cpu`. A missing font or glyph is an error.

- `motion-fonts`, `motion fonts fetch` and `motion import --fetch-fonts`: the fonts a scene lacks are found in open-licensed sources (each family's repository and Google Fonts), pinned in a lock beside the scene and kept in a content-addressed cache. For an import, each family takes the release whose glyph ids and widths match the project's stored text. A lock in a scene's `fonts` loads its faces from the cache and checks their hashes; a render never fetches (D39).
- The text check compares glyph ids with those the project stored, and the import report says when they differ.
- Font fallback at import: a font the import does not have is replaced by the closest available face (the same family, else the built-in Inter, at the same slant and nearest weight; a missing font's style is read from its PostScript name's suffix), and each use is reported as `missing fonts, substituted (`<font> → <face>`)`. `motion import` and `motion fidelity` substitute; scenes written by hand still fail on an unknown font ([D40](docs/design/decisions.md#d40-missing-fonts-are-substituted-at-import)).
- Quoted edit values take `\n` (new line) and `\t` (tab) escapes besides `\"` and `\\`, so `title text="Make\nit move"` sets two lines.
- Image layers: `"image":"photos/beach.jpg"` with a `box` draws a PNG or JPEG file stretched over the box, relative to the scene file. The engine decodes each file once, when a frame first shows it; `motion-raster` gains `Picture` and the `ImageDraw` leaf, decoding PNG with `png` and JPEG with `zune-jpeg` ([D41](docs/design/decisions.md#d41-image-layers-name-their-file-and-carry-their-box)).
- The import draws still footage: it looks for each footage file where the project stored it, then by name under the project's folder, and writes the pictures it finds as image layers. Footage it cannot find, video, image sequences and audio are reported with why. `motion_engine::import_scene` takes where the scene will be saved, so footage paths are relative to it.

- `motion-expr`: expressions compile once and run at any time over a host (comps, layers, properties, keys), with the language's array arithmetic, interpolation, loops, vector and colour functions, the legacy engine's forms, deterministic maths and randomness, and a fitter that turns per-frame samples into a few keys within a tolerance. Every distinct expression of the local corpus compiles (D38).
- `motion import` bakes expressions (D38): each one switched on is evaluated at every frame its layer shows, where the scene shows its comp, over the project read as the language sees it (comps, layers, groups, properties, effect parameters at their default, a layer's scale and opacity in percent), with the expressions it reads evaluated first. The samples are fitted into keys that replace those under it; a constant becomes a still value, source text sets the text. The report says, per expression, its engine, frames, keys before and after and largest error, or why it was not baked. Projects now record their expression engine (`ExEn`).
- `motion export <scene> --project <file.aep> --out <file.aep>` writes an edited scene back into the project it was imported from (D45): each changed static value the project stores (numbers, points, scales, opacities, colours, effect parameters) is patched into its bytes, every other byte is kept, and the patched project is read back and imported before it is saved. An unedited scene exports to the original file byte for byte. Every other change is reported with why it is not written back (a default, an expression, keys, text, a new layer).
- The import report keeps each expression's whole source, and every expression control with its values, for live links (D44).
- Curve families: `<shape>-<family>` names, such as `out-expo` or `in-out-quint`, with shapes `in`, `out`, `in-out` and `out-in`, and families `quad`, `cubic`, `quart`, `quint`, `sine`, `circ`, `expo`, `back`, `elastic` and `bounce`. Parameters go in parentheses: `in-out(2.5)`, `out-back(2)`, `out-elastic(1.2,.4)`. Also `steps(n)` and `steps(n,start)`. They are evaluated exactly at every frame, and behaviours take them too (`pop 18f out-back(2)`).
- Spring curves: `spring(bounce)` and `spring(bounce,perceived)`, a damped spring released from rest that settles on the end value, overshooting for a bounce above 0. In a key span it is scaled to the span. A behaviour without a duration lasts until it rests (`pop spring(.4,10f)`), and that settle time is solved exactly rather than by stepping.
- Labels and relative times: a composition's `labels` name moments of its timeline (`"cta":"6s"`, `"outro":"cta+2s"`), and a layer's `offset` and `span` may be written against a label, `<` (where the layer before ends) or `<<` (where it starts), moved by a time (`"span":"intro-cta"`, `"offset":"<-6f"`). They resolve when the scene is read, and `Comp::labels` keeps the resolved labels.
- Relative values and value lists: a key may be written against the property's resting value (`rest`, `rest+40`, `rest-0,40`, `rest*1.2`), resolved after layout so the move follows the layer, and the string form chains values (`"0 -> 200@40% -> 150 @0-30f"`) that split the span evenly unless a value says where it falls.
- Stagger on text parts: a text animator's `stagger` lets the parts counted by `by` take turns, each seeing the animator, selectors included, as it was its wait earlier, in `order` (`first`, `last`, `center`, `edges`, or `random` by `seed`). A text layer's `in` takes a part word, `stagger <time>` and an order (`"fade-up word 10f out-back stagger 3f"`) and becomes such an animator, so each part makes the whole entrance with its own curve.
- Trim: a `trim` shape item cuts the outlines listed before it, those in earlier groups included, down to a stretch from `start` to `end`, moved by `offset` in degrees, each on its own or `joined` as one line in order. Every paint that draws a cut outline draws the cut one, and inner groups' trims cut first. The import maps trim paths onto it (65 of the 241 local sample projects use them).

### Fixed

- An empty path reads back: the format wrote the path with no vertex as `""` and then rejected it, so a project with a path of no vertices (a shape group whose path was cleared) imported into a scene the format refused. As in SVG, `""` is now the path with no vertex, which draws nothing. On the local corpus, one sample failed to import this way; it now measures at 5.65% failing from when it has settled.
- The import reads the adjustment switch on shape and text layers, not only on solids: the layer's content says where its effects apply to what is below it, and without an effect the scene draws it draws nothing. Seven corpus samples hold 400 such shape layers, which drew as plain shapes. Mean settled error over them 0.369 → 0.329; m771 0.466 → 0.196, where a white panel hid the title.
- A null whose anchor the project leaves out rests it at its top-left corner, not its centre, so the layers it parents are no longer 50 px up and left of where the project draws them. On the local corpus, 10 samples draw closer to their reference and none passes or fails because of it; on two local `.mogrt` templates the layers now land on the preview's.
- A template's point control that drives a position kept as separate dimensions no longer writes the composition's centre over the layer's own position: the position stays as it is, and the report names the control as left out.

### Changed

- Reports name layers by address ([D43](docs/design/decisions.md#d43-one-layer-address-and-edits-per-instance)): `StoredText::layer` and `TextCheck::layer` are an `Address` where the scene writes the layer (`title`, or `scene.comps.card.layers[title]`), replacing `comp` and `layer`. `diff --ignore` and the fidelity manifest's `ignore` take addresses, so `card/photo` hides a layer in one instance. The import's own path `Step` is gone: sources use `motion_format::Step`, `address::json_at` and `address::key_text`, which the edit crate and `Place` share too.
- The variants design reads as motion works now ([D43](docs/design/decisions.md#d43-one-layer-address-and-edits-per-instance)): the project is imported directly, expressions are baked, instances of a composition are edited one at a time, and a variant can be patched back into its project. The glossary gains address, instance and override.
- Edits take a layer's address ([D43](docs/design/decisions.md#d43-one-layer-address-and-edits-per-instance)): `card/title text="Sale"` writes `overrides` on `card` for that instance alone, starting a change inside a key from the key as the instance draws it. A key path may pick a list element by id or index, `fx[glow].radius` or `scene comps.label.layers[title].text=Hi`. `none` through an instance takes back its own value, and refuses a key its composition gives. `Edit::target` is an `Address`, and `KeyPath` has steps instead of segments. `check` gives fix hints for inner layers as edits of their instance.
- `check` and `at` reach every layer at any depth ([D43](docs/design/decisions.md#d43-one-layer-address-and-edits-per-instance)): a layer inside a composition is checked in scene space and reported by its address, `card/title`, measured by what its compositions' clips let it draw. `motion at` takes addresses, and lists every layer at any depth when given none. `Violation::id` is now `Violation::address`.
- `motion import` clamps a value outside its property's range into it, as the original draws it, instead of failing: a wiggled opacity past 100%, a negative roundness, colour channels above 1. Expression samples are clamped frame by frame before the fit; the report lists each clamp under `clamped`.
- The scene's root is a composition ([D43](docs/design/decisions.md#d43-one-layer-address-and-edits-per-instance)): `Scene::root` and `SceneGraph::root` hold its canvas, clock, background and layers, and one function validates and compiles the root and every composition under `comps`.
- A diagnostic's path is a `Place` ([D43](docs/design/decisions.md#d43-one-layer-address-and-edits-per-instance)): an `Address` inside the scene, or text for places outside it. Errors name a layer by its id, as edits and queries do: `title.in`, not `layers[0].in`. A layer whose id is missing or shared is still named by its index.
- `motion export` compares what it reads back with the edit in the import's six-decimal steps, allowing one, so a value half a step from both neighbours no longer fails the check.
- Baking expressions sits behind `motion-import`'s `bake` feature, which only the CLI turns on: `motion-engine` renders and imports without linking the script engine. The D38 and D39 guards check each crate's resolved dependencies (`cargo tree`), not its manifest lines.
- The bake eases the values it reads with the scene's own curves and motion paths (`motion_scene::Ease`, `PathSpan`), not a copy of them.
- An expression that reads nothing that changes with time runs once, not at every frame: a run knows whether it read `time`, a value the host says changes, or randomness seeded by the time (`Run::constant`, `Host::constant`).
- Six crates instead of ten: the error type and shared grammar pieces live in `motion-format`, evaluation in `motion-scene`, and logging setup in `motion-engine`.
- `check` evaluates one frame at a time instead of keeping every frame in memory.
- Error messages name the path and the accepted form, with an example, for every syntax and unknown-name error.
- Shorthands (size, time, colour, rect, font, position, curves, `in`, `anim` tracks) are read by `winnow` grammars.
- The scene is read with a serde document, so errors in its shape carry the path of the value.
- Edit paths use a real grammar, so `card anim.opacity="0 -> 1 @0-12f"` and JSON values (`anim={...}`) are read by `serde_json`.
- Quiet by default: nothing is logged unless `--log` or `MOTION_LOG` asks for it, and a failing run prints only its `error:` line.
- `motion diff` and `motion fidelity` measure a third window, `content`: where the scene draws or the reference differs from the scene's background by more than the tolerance, so content the scene fails to draw counts against it. It is the headline number in their output, their JSON and the ledger, and the ledger's regression check counts it; `DiffOptions` takes the layers to ignore, and the diff leaves out where they draw ([D42](docs/design/decisions.md#d42-fidelity-is-headlined-over-the-reference-content)).

- `at` prints only the properties that differ from their default after `x y w h`.
- The property `x` and `y` are now the components of `position`; `scale` is a point (`scale=0.8,0.8` in `at`); `at` places the layer's anchor, which rests at the centre of its box, so existing scenes draw as before.
- `check` fix hints skip axes driven by `anim`.
- Properties live in one registry per kind of object (`LAYER`, `GROUP`, and one per shape item and effect); unknown-property errors name the kind (`unknown layer property`).
- A layer with two content keys reports the conflict at the second key.
- Text is set with `motion-text` at compile time, so boxes, `at` and `check` measure real glyphs; `compile` takes a `Typesetter` and returns a `Result`, and `ApproxTextMeasure` is gone. A text layer's own coordinates start at its first baseline, and its box is the layout's bounds. The built-in font's family is `Inter Variable`.
- Layer boxes are rectangles (`kurbo::Rect`): rests and `at` resolve in them. `Session::graph`, `frame` and `check` return a `Result`, and the session has a `fonts` step.
- Layer `fill` takes any colour form, including `[r, g, b, a]` from 0 to 1.
- `NodeState` carries what a node draws (`content`, `local_bounds`, `fill`, `fx`, `hidden`); `check` ignores hidden layers.
- Unit tests moved out of `src/` into `tests/unit`, mirroring the source tree.

### Fixed

- The import measures an effect's points on a shape or text layer from the layer's origin, which sits in the middle of the composition-sized box the project measures them in, not at its top left. Ramps, four-colour gradients, fractal noise, turbulent displacement and the transform effect on such layers now land where the original draws them: mean settled content error over 193 samples 0.2956 → 0.2944, 7 better and 3 worse by at most 0.006 (m647 0.162 → 0.042, m556 0.120 → 0.045).
